In a real conversation you don't repeat yourself: after asking about someone, you ask "what about her projects?". That's easy for a person and hard for a search engine, because "her projects" on its own doesn't say whose. This page explains how LocalCortex handles follow-up questions.

## The conversation the model sees

Along with the passages from your documents, the model is shown the **last 6 messages** of the chat (long answers are shortened to 800 characters). That's what lets it understand "her" or "that policy" when it writes the answer.

Two exceptions:

- If the search found nothing relevant, the history is **left out** and the model gets the plain "I couldn't find it" prompt. Otherwise an off-topic question could get an answer spun from the earlier conversation.
- Answers that were stopped halfway or failed are shown in the chat but **never** sent back to the model.

## Step 1: does the question refer back?

Before searching, LocalCortex checks the question with a simple word check. No AI is involved, so it takes no time. A question counts as a follow-up that needs rewriting if:

- it contains a **referring word**: *he, him, his, she, her, hers, they, them, their, it, its, this, that, these, those, one, ones, there, same, former, latter*
- **or** it starts with *And…, Also…, So…, What about…, How about…*

| Question | Referring word? | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| Tell me more about **her**. | yes | sent to the model to be rewritten |
| **What about** the second project? | yes | sent to the model to be rewritten |
| **And** after five years? | yes | sent to the model to be rewritten |
| Can I carry over what I don't use? | no | searched together with the previous question ([step 3](#step-3-when-theres-nothing-to-rewrite)) |
| What is the capital of France? | no | never sent to the rewriter |

The first question in a chat is always searched as it is. There's nothing earlier for it to refer to.

## Step 2: rewrite it into a standalone question

A question that refers back is given to `llama3`, along with the recent conversation, and turned into a question that makes sense on its own:

> **You:** Who is ABC?  
> **You:** Tell me more about **her**.  
> **Searched as:** Tell me more about **ABC**.

The rewritten question is then searched exactly like a first question, with the normal 0.63 threshold. The model still answers your own words; the rewrite is only used for the search.

So the rewriter has guard rails:

1. **Only questions that refer back are sent to it** (step 1). "What is the capital of France?" has no referring word, so it's never rewritten.
2. **Refusals are removed** from the conversation it sees. They contain no names to resolve, and they confused it.
3. **Its output is checked.** A rewrite is thrown away if it drops more than 40% of the question's own key words (it swapped in a different question), or if it's much longer than the question (it answered instead of rewriting). Step 3 is used instead.

## Step 3: when there's nothing to rewrite

If a follow-up has no referring word, or the rewrite failed or was rejected, LocalCortex searches with the **previous question and the new one together**. "How many vacation days do I get?" followed by "Can I carry over what I don't use?" is searched as both sentences, so the search knows it's about vacation.

This path has **two checks**, not one:

1. The combined question must reach the normal threshold, **0.63**.
2. The new question **on its own** must reach **0.57**, just above the highest score any general-knowledge question got during calibration.

Without the second check, an off-topic follow-up borrows relevance from the question before it:

"What is the capital of France?", asked right after a real question about the documents, scored **0.479** on its own but **0.682** combined with the previous question. That would have cleared 0.63 and sent the model unrelated passages.

| New question alone | 4 | 0 |
| Combined, one check | 12 | **6** |
| Combined, both checks (default) | 10 | **0** |

The two follow-ups the default misses are very vague ("Can I carry over what I don't use?"). On their own they score lower than some off-topic questions. On the larger 4-document test set, the default gets 12 of 13 with no off-topic leaks.
